The honest version

Most of these projects fail. I've been on both kinds.

The industry has quoted an eighty-percent failure rate for as long as I've been in it, and my own record roughly agrees. I can't change the odds just by turning up; nobody honest can promise that. What I can bring is the pattern recognition of someone who has watched both kinds of project from the inside, and a preference for the gloomy estimate that holds over the cheerful one that doesn't.

How I work

Things I ask about before saying yes.

Less a list of demands than a description of where I've been useful. Engagements that go well tend to share these, so I'd rather ask early than discover late.

  • Something tangible reaches production. Advisory-only work isn't where I'm at my best.
  • Version control for everything, automated deployment, small releases — DORA, more or less.
  • A real IDE, Git, and AI-assisted coding where it helps.
  • A Mac-friendly environment.
  • Agile as a practice rather than a ceremony; documentation in Markdown, not .docx.
  • Platform decisions made by the engineers who will live with them.
  • A budget or rate range stated before the first call, so neither of us wastes an afternoon.
  • No permanent position, and no managing people as a job.

I have managed people — fourteen months as Head of EU Data Engineering at a large bank, five hundred-odd people going through an Agile adoption. It taught me a great deal, mostly about which half of leadership I'm suited to. I lead by doing the work and then handing it over.
